# Check your property manager yourself (free)

You don't need to send anyone your documents. This checklist and prompt let you run the same check in your own AI tool. Not legal advice: treat what it finds as questions for your manager.

## Step 1: find these clauses in your management agreement

Write down what yours says for each:

| Clause | What to write down |
|---|---|
| Management fee | The percentage, and whether it's on rent **collected** or rent **due** |
| Other fees | Leasing, renewal, inspection, maintenance markup or "coordination" fee |
| Repair approval limit | The amount the manager can spend without asking you |
| Emergencies | What counts, and how quickly they must tell you |
| Inspections | How often, who does them, and whether you get photos |
| Owner payments | The date you should be paid each month, and any reserve kept |
| Renewals | How far ahead they must propose a rent, and whether you approve it |
| Invoices | Whether you can see vendor invoices, and where |

## Step 2: gather your last 3 to 12 monthly statements

Black out tenant names, addresses and account numbers first (see `redaction-guide.md`).

## Step 3: paste this prompt into Claude or ChatGPT with the documents

```
You are helping a rental property owner check their monthly owner statements
against their property management agreement. You are not giving legal advice.

1. From the agreement, list every term about: management fee (and whether it is
   on rent collected or rent due), other fees and markups, the repair approval
   limit, emergency rules and notice time, inspection frequency and who performs
   them, the owner payment date and reserve, and renewal rent proposals.
   Quote the clause number for each.

2. Go through every statement line by line and check it against those terms.
   Recalculate every percentage fee yourself. Flag:
   - any fee charged at a different rate or on a different base than agreed
   - any single repair over the approval limit not marked as an emergency
   - repeat repairs to the same item or unit within 90 days
   - inspection fees where the description suggests the manager did not inspect
   - owner payments after the agreed date
   - renewals or new leases with no sign of the required proposal
   - charges for anything the agreement does not mention

3. For each item, give: what the statement shows, what the agreement says
   (with clause number), the dollar difference if any, and the exact question
   to ask the manager. Describe each as a difference to ask about, not a breach.

4. List what you could not check from these documents.

5. Draft a short, polite email to the manager asking all the questions.
```

## Step 4: check its maths

AI tools make arithmetic mistakes. Recalculate any dollar amount before you send the email.
