## 1. Exit Kit (working name) — 22/25

**Pitch:** A one-time-purchase tool that pulls everything out of a product whose new owner just multiplied the price, keeps it as a searchable local archive, and loads it into the replacement you choose. Start with Harvest; Mailchimp is next.

**Gap it fills:** Bending Spoons buys established software and raises prices sharply. Harvest customers report 10x bills this month. The same company agreed to buy Mailchimp on November 19 and has bought MileIQ and Airtable. Customers want out, but leaving means losing years of invoices, time entries and client records, or rebuilding them by hand. Replacement vendors publish "migrate to us" guides for their own product only. I found no independent tool that does the whole exit.

**Who has the pain:** Freelancers, consultants and agencies of 1 to 20 people on Harvest. The moment: the renewal email arrives showing the new price, and they have until the renewal date to leave.

**Proof:**

1. "Bending Spoons tried to 2x my Harvest bill on the usage-based plan and 146x (yes, that's not a percentage) on the unlimited plan. I switched to Clockify [1] and it works well." — mherrmann, 2026-11-09, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50421498)
2. "I knew it was going to be more expensive, but when I got the bill this month and saw the price was over $200 and then noticed the Bending Spoons LLC tagline at the bottom, that was the end. Claude is backing up all of my data via the API and will be vibe coding my next invoice." — tcdent, 2026-11-09, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50420826)
3. "Given that Mailchimp has now received the Bending Spoons kiss of death, what are easy-to-migrate-to alternatives?" — ablation, 2026-11-20, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50574381)

Supporting signal:

- The thread "RIP, Harvest" has 450 points and 154 comments ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50420035)). In it and the two Mailchimp threads, 50 different people wrote about switching, exporting or building a replacement.
- One customer quotes their renewal email: "$40.00/month with 5 seats" becoming "$586.50/month" ([Digory](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50421043)).
- Why the hassle matters: "they can raise prices 8-10x, but only maybe 60-80% of their customers will cancel because it's a big hassle" ([cameldrv](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50421076)).
- Outside HN: a Mailchimp user wants "to bail out sooner rather than later given the track record of Bending Spoons" ([Mac Power Users](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/mailchimp-open-source-alternatives-now-that-bending-spoons-has-bought-it/48475/1)); a MileIQ user says "Going to have to find a new solution now!" ([DecoPerson](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50421071)).
- Secondary reports describe 10x to 15x Harvest increases ([lavx.hu](https://news.lavx.hu/article/bending-spoons-raises-harvest-prices-tenfold-replaces-support-with-ai), [tillage.ai](https://tillage.ai/blog/harvest-price-increase-2026)). Not verified against Harvest's own pricing page.

**Score:** frequency 5, intensity 5, willingness to pay 4, reachability 4, buildability 4.

**Closest competitors and why they miss:**

| Competitor | What it does | Why it misses |
|---|---|---|
| Harvest's own export | Data export from inside the product | One commenter used it as the spec for a hand-built replacement; it gives you files, not a working archive or an import elsewhere. I have not tested what it leaves out |
| Zebu (€5/mo time tracker) | Has "a full Harvest migration" | Only into Zebu. Its founder says the hard part is "marketing/gaining traction" |
| Clockify, Toggl and other trackers | Replace Harvest | Each imports into itself; invoice history and PDFs are generally not their concern (unverified per vendor) |
| Vendor migration guides (for example Softr's Airtable guide) | Step-by-step moves to that vendor | One destination each, written to win the customer |
| Do it yourself with a coding agent | Several commenters did this | Takes days; one reported about 10 days ([joefitzgerald](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50421385)) |

A search for an independent "exit" or migration kit for these products found none. General data-migration services exist (I did not check Import2 or similar); verify before building.

**Pricing guess (estimate):** $49 one-time per Harvest account, $99 for agencies with more than 5 seats. Reasoning: the buyer is avoiding a bill that rose by $100 to $500 a month, wants to pay once, and is angry about subscriptions.

**MVP:**

- **Core feature:** command-line tool plus a small local web view. Enter a Harvest API token; it downloads clients, projects, tasks, time entries, invoices with PDFs, expenses with receipts and estimates into SQLite and plain files. The local view lets you search and re-download any invoice after you cancel.
- **Importers:** two destinations for version one. Pick them by asking the first ten customers; Clockify and one self-hosted option are the likely pair.
- **Skip:** a hosted service, your own time tracker, team accounts, Mailchimp (week 3 or later), anything that writes back to Harvest.
- **Stack:** TypeScript or Python CLI, SQLite, a static local viewer. No server, so no customer data ever reaches you.

**14-day plan:**

| Days | Work |
|---|---|
| 1 | Run the risk test below before writing code |
| 2 to 5 | Full Harvest export through the API, with rate-limit handling and resume |
| 6 to 7 | Local archive viewer: search, invoice PDFs, totals by client and year |
| 8 to 10 | First importer |
| 11 to 12 | Second importer; a verification report that counts records on both sides |
| 13 | Landing page titled for the search people are already making: "leave Harvest without losing your invoices" |
| 14 | Hand-hold 3 customers through a real exit |

**First 10 customers:**

- Commenters in the Harvest thread who said they are leaving or already left. Use the contact in their HN profile, or reply in the thread.
- The Zebu founder, as a partner rather than a customer: he needs distribution, you need a destination.
- Freelancer and agency communities where Harvest renewals are being discussed.
- People searching "Harvest alternative"; one commenter says those searches have jumped (unverified).

Message: "You said you're leaving Harvest after the price change. I built a tool that pulls out everything, including invoice PDFs, keeps it searchable on your own machine and loads it into [tool]. $49 once. Want to try it on your account before your renewal date?"

**Biggest risk:** Harvest's built-in export plus a free importer at the destination is already good enough, so nobody pays.

**Cheapest test this week:** Open a free Harvest trial, add sample data, run the built-in export, and try to import it into Clockify and Zebu. Write down what is lost. If nothing important is lost, stop. If invoices, PDFs, rates or expenses go missing, post that list where Harvest customers are and ask who wants the fix; five replies is a go.

### WHAT WOULD KILL THIS IDEA?

- **Is it really unknown?** Partly. The pain is widely known. An independent tool is what I could not find, and I did not check general migration services.
- **Would a stranger pay in week one?** Plausible: they are escaping a much larger bill on a deadline. Unproven until the test above.
- **Is every quote real and linked?** Yes. All were copied by script from the source pages.
- **Could a big company copy it in a weekend?** Any replacement vendor can build its own importer, and several have. None has a reason to build exits to its rivals.
- **The wave is short.** Harvest customers renew over the next 12 months, then the demand is gone. The business only lasts if you move to the next product each time: Mailchimp, MileIQ, Airtable.
- **You depend on the old vendor's API.** Harvest could restrict tokens or rate limits for customers who are leaving.
- **One-time revenue.** Fifty customers at $49 is about $2,450. This is a small business unless the Mailchimp and Airtable waves are much larger.
- **Coding agents.** Your buyers include developers who would rather ask an agent to do it. The non-developers in agencies are the real market.

## 2. Supplier price-rise catcher — 17/25, below the bar

**Do not build yet.** One strong complaint, weak frequency.

**Pitch:** Read each supplier invoice, compare every line with the last cost in Shopify, and list only the lines where the cost rose, with the new shelf price needed to hold margin.

**Who has the pain:** Independent retailers with 1 to 3 shops and many suppliers, when a delivery arrives.

**Proof:**

1. "When stock arrives, we manually check each invoice line against the cost in Shopify. If a price has gone up, we work out the new retail price to hold our margin, update Shopify, tell both shops, and print new shelf labels, It's v slow/mnaual and price rises slip through." — WB-LS, 2026-11-12, [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/two-pet-shops-on-shopify-pos-how-do-you-handle-supplier-invoices-and-repricing/714773/1) (thread: 40 posts, 68 likes)

I found only one merchant saying this in their own words. Two related threads were started by people building tools, which is research, not demand.

**Score:** frequency 2, intensity 4, willingness to pay 4, reachability 3, buildability 4.

**Closest competitors:** FyreTrail (suggested in the thread), Sumtracker (the merchant already uses it), and Shopify's own purchase orders, which a reply says now show the last price paid and accept invoice lines by CSV.

**Cheapest test:** find five more independent retailers describing this unprompted. If you cannot, it stays dead.

### WHAT WOULD KILL THIS IDEA?

Shopify's purchase orders already cover most of it, and the evidence is one shop.

## 3. Classic Anki client for iOS — 18/25, fails the gap check

**Do not build.** Listed because it was the single loudest complaint of the run.

**Pitch:** An Anki-compatible iPhone app with the dense, old layout.

**Who has the pain:** Medical and language students who review hundreds of cards a day, after the 26.10 update.

**Proof:**

1. "Hi, my Anki app automatically updated last night. I really just hate the new format so much. All of my decks won't even fit on one page" — a_aga13, 2026-11-09, [Anki Forums](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/undo-26-10-update/74279/1) (thread: 113 posts, 757 likes)
2. "The new interface not only looks clustered and is overflowing with infos, it's also a pain to use. Especially the search function has become nearly impossible to work with." — Xkcd, 2026-11-10, [Anki Forums](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/undo-26-10-update/74279/7)
3. "I use anki for med school and I really need it everyday. The new update has broken several integral features such as image occlusion" — maxw, 2026-11-11, [Anki Forums](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/i-want-a-refund/74345/1)

**Score:** frequency 5, intensity 4, willingness to pay 3, reachability 5, buildability 1.

### WHAT WOULD KILL THIS IDEA?

- A full Anki-compatible client is months of work, not two weeks.
- The vendor can end the demand with one "classic layout" switch; a forum reply already suggests they might.
- The official iOS app is closed source, and I did not check whether AnkiWeb's terms allow third-party clients.
- Open-source Anki-compatible iOS apps were posted to Hacker News this month (Amgi, Kioku).
