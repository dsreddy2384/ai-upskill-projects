## 1. Clone Watch: early warning and takedown packets for indie games that get cloned

**Pitch:** Give it your Steam page. Every day it checks Steam, itch.io and Roblox for new games that copy your title, description, capsule art or screenshots. If it finds one, it emails you a side-by-side comparison and a takedown packet for that store, ready to file.

### The gap

AI tools have made it quick to clone an indie demo. During Steam Next Fest in October 2026 (8,000+ demos), clone stories spread through the indie game-dev forums. Some developers are now withholding demos, the main way an indie game builds wishlists. Today their defence is manual: search Steam and Google, compare follower charts on SteamDB, fill in each store's form by hand. The tools that exist are either for buyers (Clone Report, a free crowd-reported list and browser extension) or for large brands (UpGuard, Red Points, Irdeto/Denuvo enforcement, all quote-based). I found no tool, at a price an indie developer would pay, that watches the stores for clones of their own game and prepares the filing.

### Who has the pain

- **Role:** solo developers and studios of 1–5 people with a Steam "Coming Soon" page, a public demo or playtest, or a game that trended on social media.
- **Moment:** (a) just before publishing a demo or joining Next Fest, when they are deciding whether it is safe to show the game; (b) the day they find a clone, when they need evidence and the right form fast.
- **Also:** small mobile studios whose games are copied on Google Play and the App Store. That is the harder case, so it is out of v1.

### Closest competitors and why they miss

| Product | What it does | Why it misses |
|---|---|---|
| Clone Report (clonereport.com), primary, read tonight | Free crowd-reported clone database (188 reported clones, 25,000 originals) and a browser extension that warns *buyers* on Steam, PlayStation, Xbox, eShop, App Store, Play and itch.io | Reactive and aimed at buyers: a clone only appears after someone reports it and a moderator approves it. No developer alerts, no takedown packet. **It is also the most likely to add this feature quickly** (see the kill section) |
| UpGuard App Store Threat Detection (beta), secondary | Searches Apple and Google stores for a brand's name and compiles evidence | Enterprise security product; app stores only, no Steam, itch.io or Roblox; does not file takedowns |
| Red Points, Irdeto (Denuvo) enforcement, Muso, Bolster/Deepinfo-type brand protection, secondary | Piracy and brand monitoring, takedowns | Quote-based enterprise pricing, focused on piracy sites and big IP, not small Steam games |
| Undriel ShadowShield (Wix app), secondary | Clone alerts and takedown claims for online-store products, from $19.99/month for 5 products | Different market (e-commerce product pages), but a useful price anchor |
| Manual: SteamDB, Google, Steam's copyright form | Free | Slow; this is the pain the quotes describe |

### Pricing guess (estimate)

- **Free:** one-time scan of one game. This is the lead magnet.
- **$9/month:** one game, daily Steam and itch.io scan, email alerts.
- **$29/month:** up to 5 games, adds Roblox, weekly digest, and takedown packets.
- **$29 one-off "I've been cloned" packet** for developers who find a clone before they sign up.

Reasoning: ShadowShield's $19.99 for 5 products and the cost of a cheap Steam marketing tool put a ceiling near $30 for small teams. Enterprise brand protection is far above that, which leaves room underneath. The one-off packet tests willingness to pay at the moment the pain is sharpest.

### MVP

- **Core feature:** daily job per watched game. (1) Query Steam store search with title n-grams and top tags. (2) Pull appdetails for new candidates. (3) Score each candidate on title similarity, description embedding similarity, and perceptual-hash distance across capsule art and screenshots. (4) Email anything above the threshold, showing the two games side by side.
- **Takedown packet:** archive both pages (Wayback Save Page Now), record dates (Steam "coming soon" date, earliest Wayback copy, first public post), include the comparison images, and prefill the text for Steam's copyright notice form, itch.io (support@itch.io) and Roblox's DMCA form. Include a plain-language warning that resemblance is not infringement and that false claims carry liability under 17 USC 512(f). **No legal advice; get a lawyer to review the template before charging for it.**
- **Skip in v1:** App Store and Google Play, automatic filing, trademark help, Chinese storefronts, video and trailer matching.
- **Stack:** Python (requests, imagehash, rapidfuzz, a small sentence-transformer model run locally), Postgres (Supabase), a cron worker (Fly.io or Render), Resend for email, Stripe Checkout, and a one-page Next.js or plain HTML site.

**14-day plan**
- Days 1–2: Steam search and appdetails client; candidate store; respect rate limits.
- Days 3–4: similarity scoring. Test against known cases: Needle In A Haystack Simulator vs Find The Needle (from the HTMAG post), and the Escape the Walls clones PC Gamer reported.
- Day 5: tune the threshold on 30 random games so false positives stay low.
- Days 6–7: itch.io search and Roblox experience search (best effort).
- Days 8–9: evidence packet generator (PDF with archived links and prefilled form text).
- Day 10: email alerts and a sign-up page (paste a Steam URL, get a free scan).
- Day 11: Stripe for $9, $29 and the one-off packet.
- Days 12–13: run free scans for the first 10 developers by hand.
- Day 14: write up the hit rate (how many scans found anything real) and decide whether to continue.

### Biggest risk and the cheapest test this week

**Risk:** most scans find nothing, because harmful clones cluster around games that are already viral. That would leave a monitoring product with nothing to report and customers who cancel. Willingness to pay is the weak score (3).

**Cheapest test (about 6 hours, $0):** search by hand (Steam search, itch.io, Roblox) for 15 Next Fest demos with fewer than 5,000 followers plus the 3 known clone cases, and count the real hits. Then offer the free report to 20 developers through public replies or DMs. **Pass:** at least 3 of 18 games have a credible clone **and** at least 2 developers either pay for the $29 packet or reply asking for monitoring. **Fail:** fewer than 2 hits. In that case pivot to the one-off "I've been cloned" packet only, or drop the idea.

### WHAT WOULD KILL THIS IDEA?

- **Is it really unknown?** Only partly. Clone Report covers buyers and is community-run. Enterprise vendors cover big brands. I searched for "clone alert", "copycat monitor", "app store threat detection", "brand protection for games" and "game clone takedown service", and found no self-serve, developer-side product for Steam, itch.io and Roblox. That conclusion rests on web search only; I could not check Product Hunt or Reddit, which are blocked.
- **Would a stranger pay in week one?** Doubtful for monitoring. More plausible for the one-off packet bought by a developer who has just been cloned. The test above measures this directly.
- **Is every quote real and linked?** Yes. All four quotes and the counter-quote were copied by script from the forum JSON or the HN Algolia text and appended to pain-database.csv with links. The trade-press figures (8,000+ demos, Escape the Walls clones) are secondary, from PC Gamer and GamesRadar as reported by search results; I did not open those articles.
- **Could a big company copy it in a weekend?** Clone Report could add a "watch my game" email alert in a weekend; it already has the database and moderation. Valve said at Steam Indie Connect (secondary: esports.gg, page refused my fetch) that it is not adding new AI rules, so Valve shipping this next month looks unlikely. The defensible part is the takedown packet and coverage of Roblox and itch.io, and that is a thin moat.
