## 1. PMA Watch (working name): 20/25

**Pitch:** Every month, check that your property manager did what your management agreement says, and get a ready-to-send email listing what they missed.

**The gap:** Out-of-state owners pay a manager around 8 to 10% of rent (a common range; estimate, not verified tonight) and get back a monthly statement. The statement can add up correctly while the manager breaks the agreement: repairs over the approval limit without asking, "inspections" that were the tenant's own phone photos, money held for months, renewals priced for the manager's convenience. Owner portals (AppFolio, Buildium, Rentec, Rentvine) are run by the manager and show what the manager chooses to show. DoorVault's Knox reads statements and checks rent, the fee percentage, duplicate expenses and the net payout. Its pages say nothing about approval limits, inspections, work-order notice or renewal pricing. Contracko reviews a management agreement once, before you sign. **Nobody I found turns the agreement into a monthly checklist and checks the manager against it.**

**Who has the pain:** individual investors with 1 to 20 doors, usually bought out of state through a turnkey seller, managed by a local firm. The moment it bites is the monthly statement, a surprise repair bill, or the renewal decision 60 to 90 days before a lease ends.

**Pricing guess:** $12/month for up to 3 doors, $29/month for up to 15 doors, billed annually. Reasoning (estimates): manager fees on a $2,100/month rent run about $170 to $210/month, so $12 is under 10% of what the owner already pays to be managed. It has to sit near DoorVault's $6/door so it doesn't look more expensive than the broader tool. One caught over-limit repair pays for a year. Optional $49 one-off "agreement setup and first 3-month review" as the way in.

**MVP:**

- **Core feature:** upload the agreement PDF, and an LLM pulls out the terms (approval limit, inspection frequency and who performs it, payout date, fee schedule, notice duties, renewal process) into a checklist the owner can edit. Each month the owner forwards the statement and work-order emails to a private inbound address. The tool checks them against the checklist, flags repeat repairs per unit and late payouts, and produces a one-page brief plus a polite draft email to the manager.
- **Skip:** bank connections, bookkeeping, tax reports, a mobile app, scraping manager portals, scoring managers publicly, anything that sounds like legal advice.
- **Stack:** Next.js + Supabase (Postgres, auth, storage), Postmark inbound email, PDF text extraction (pdfplumber, OCR fallback), the Claude API for term extraction and the brief, Stripe. A renewal-rent check against comps (for example the RentCast API) comes later.
- **14-day plan:**
  - Days 1 to 2: collect 5 real agreements and 15 statements (start with the concierge test below).
  - Days 3 to 5: agreement-term extraction with an editable checklist.
  - Days 6 to 8: statement and work-order parsing for AppFolio and Buildium statement formats.
  - Days 9 to 10: rule checks, repeat-repair detection, one-page brief.
  - Day 11: draft email to the manager.
  - Day 12: Stripe and onboarding.
  - Days 13 to 14: run it on the 5 concierge owners' real months and fix what breaks.

**First 10 customers:** the owners in these threads (Steven Harris, Travis Cooke, Kevin S., Manas M., and Anitha B., who says she is "going thru a similar experience") through BiggerPockets messages, then a "here's what I checked on 3 months of my statements" post in the BiggerPockets Managing Your Property forum. After that, out-of-state and turnkey buyer communities (Memphis, Birmingham, Cleveland, Indianapolis markets).

Message to send:

> "Saw your thread about your PM. I'm building a small tool that reads your management agreement, then checks each month's statement and work orders against it (approval limits, who actually did the inspections, payout timing) and drafts the email to your PM. Would you let me run it on your last 3 statements for free? If it finds nothing, you've lost 10 minutes."

**Biggest risk:** DoorVault adds agreement checks, or owners decide a ChatGPT prompt is enough. Second risk: statements are PDFs in dozens of formats and work-order evidence often isn't in them, so the check may be shallow without portal access.

**Cheapest test this week:** a concierge pass for 5 owners from the threads above. Do it by hand with Claude: agreement → checklist → their last 3 statements → brief plus draft email. Success means at least 3 of 5 send in their agreement, at least 2 have a flag they act on, and at least 2 agree to pay $12/month or $49 for the next review. If fewer than 3 send documents, kill it.

**Sign-off flag:** this reads legal contracts. It should present findings as questions to ask the manager, never as legal conclusions, and a lawyer should review the disclaimer and wording before launch. No medical sign-off needed.

### WHAT WOULD KILL THIS IDEA?

- **Is it really unknown?** Only partly. DoorVault already sells owner-side statement audits, and Contracko reviews agreements before signing. The monthly check against the agreement is unclaimed as far as tonight's search shows. Searches covered owner portals, "PM audit", "PMA compliance checker" and "property manager scorecard"; none found it.
- **Would a stranger pay in week one?** Unproven. DoorVault is free for 2 doors, which anchors the price low. The owners with the strongest pain (Steven, Travis, Manas) are angry enough to try it. Whether they pay is what the concierge test answers.
- **Is every quote real and linked?** Yes. All quotes were extracted by script from BiggerPockets' own thread data and are stored with links in `pain-database.csv`. The forum has many AI-written or vendor replies, so I used only owner posts.
- **Could a big company copy it in a weekend?** DoorVault could add agreement checks in a few weeks. AppFolio and Buildium could add better owner alerts, but their customer is the manager, which is why owners can't count on those alerts today. The only moat is speed and being on the owner's side.
